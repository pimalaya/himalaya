# Delta

## ADDED Requirements

None.

## MODIFIED Requirements

### Requirement: One query language
(search) The `envelope search` query SHALL parse into a single `SearchEmailsQuery` (filter plus sort), independent of the backend. The grammar covers the `date`, `after` and `before` date clauses, `after` and `before` both strict, the `from`, `to`, `cc` and `bcc` address clauses and `address` over all four, the `subject` and `body` text clauses, the `flag` clause over any flag name, the valueless `attachment` clause and the `message-id` clause, taking a whole id with or without its angle brackets, combined with `not`, `and` and `or`, binding in that order, grouped with parentheses, and an `order by` list over date, from, to and subject. The sort comes last, introduced by the words `order by` outside any pattern, and lists at least one key. Every keyword of the grammar is a whole word. A pattern opening with a double quote is a quoted pattern, refused when unterminated; an unquoted pattern takes `\"`, `\\`, `\ `, `\(` and `\)` escapes. Blanks after `(` and before `)` are insignificant.

### Requirement: A clause a backend does not answer is refused with a code
(search) A query holding a clause the account's backend cannot compile SHALL be refused with the code `search-unsupported`; nothing is searched and no clause is dropped. A clause or sort key the backend declares `none` SHALL be refused before connecting, naming the option and the backend; one it answers in part SHALL be refused while it is translated, before any request, the message quoting the clause as the language writes it and saying why. IMAP refuses `attachment`, Gmail `flag answered` and keyword flags, Microsoft Graph every flag clause and a `message-id` clause beside any other, JMAP `message-id`.

### Requirement: Per-backend translation
(search) Each backend SHALL translate the shared query into its native surface. IMAP translates to `UID SEARCH` / `UID SORT` keys (`SENTBEFORE` for `before`, an `OR` of `FROM`, `TO`, `CC` and `BCC` for `address`), negations pushed down to single keys (double negations cancelled, De Morgan over `and` and `or`, a negated `address` sent as `NOT FROM x NOT TO x NOT CC x NOT BCC x`), a root `and` sent as the list of its keys, a `flag` naming an IANA keyword sent in its canonical spelling (`flag junk` as `KEYWORD $Junk`), and a UID the server's `SORT` lists twice kept once, with a client-side sort fallback when the server lacks `SORT` or is iCloud (provider-quirks), which orders as RFC 5256 defines (base subject, first address's mailbox, ties in sequence order). JMAP translates to a `JmapFilter` and comparator list scoped to the mailbox id; on the chain of `and` from the root a date clause over-approximates on the received date and is re-checked exactly on the sent date client-side, while under `or` or `not` it reads the received date alone, a client-side residual being unable to hold across a disjunction or a negation. Maildir and mbox list then evaluate a shared client-side matcher (`matches_filter` plus `sort_envelopes`) over the fetched envelopes, reusing the read bytes for body clauses and reading the `Cc:` and `Bcc:` headers off them only when a clause needs them. `sort_envelopes` sorts as RFC 5256 `SORT` does: `subject` on the base subject (section 2.1), `from` and `to` on the mailbox of the first address, both under `i;ascii-casemap`, ties keeping their order. pimdir evaluates the same matcher over envelopes built from the stored summaries, the copy recipients and the attachment mark coming from the summary, reading a message's local body only when the filter holds a body clause; a message whose body is not local matches no body clause. Gmail translates to a `q` value, each pattern a literal: quotes, braces and parentheses become spaces, a standalone `OR` or `AND` is lowercased, anything but a bare word is quoted, and a pattern with no word left is refused with `search-unsupported`; its sort uses the shared `sort_envelopes`; a date clause as Unix seconds bounding the UTC day (`after d` from the start of the next day, one second early so a boundary message is kept, `before d` to the start of that day), Gmail reading a bare date as midnight in PST and comparing it with the message's received date. Graph translates to a KQL `$search` (`participants` for `address`, `hasAttachments` for `attachment`), each word of a pattern a quoted KQL phrase, so a word that is KQL syntax (`OR`, `x:y`, `alpha*`) is matched as a word, the words of one pattern grouped in parentheses and a double quote dropped, and sorts the page locally, Graph refusing `$skip` and `$orderby` beside it. A `message-id` clause SHALL match the whole id, compared byte for byte with the envelope's bare `Message-ID:`: IMAP sends `HEADER Message-ID`, which the server matches as a substring, and keeps on the chain of `and` from the root only the fetched envelopes carrying the id, a substring match remaining under `or` and `not` (`partial`); Gmail sends `rfc822msgid:`; Graph answers a lone `message-id` clause with a `$filter` on `internetMessageId`, `$filter` taking no `$search` beside it (`partial`); the local evaluator compares the envelope's id; JMAP declares it `none`, io-jmap carrying no `header` condition.
